The premise

You already know how to scan. Today is about why a scan fails.

You already know the technique. Every scan error has a physical cause in optics or in a dental material. Know the cause, and you know the fix, or when to reach for an impression tray.

Recap: the must-knows BO 1

The scanner projects and records light; software builds the mesh

1
Project
The wand sends a point, a line, or a structured pattern of light onto teeth and tissue.
2
Capture
Cameras record how that light appears. The wand itself performs no calculations.
3
Calculate
Software finds the depth of every point: a point cloud of vertices with X, Y, and Z.
4
Mesh
Edges join vertices into triangles. Watertight means every edge is shared by exactly two faces.
STL carries shape only. PLY and OBJ also carry color. STL remains the file mills and printers expect.

Sources. [1] Pacheco RR, Rocha MG. Digital technologies. In: Phillips' Science of Dental Materials. 14th ed. Elsevier; 2026. Ch. 15.

Optical triangulation BO 4

A known angle between projector and camera turns position into depth

Projector, surface, and camera form a triangle with a fixed base and known angles. Move the surface, then switch to a box preparation and deepen it.

Angle at the camera0°
Depth solved3.0 mm

Confocal imaging BO 5

A confocal scanner records only what is in focus, one plane at a time

A pinhole lets light from one focal plane reach the sensor; light from above or below is filtered out. Sweep the focal plane through the preparation.

Points in focus now0
Surface captured0%
Why it works. Only a surface sitting on the focal plane returns a sharp image, so focus position becomes depth.

Sources. [1] Pacheco RR, Rocha MG. Phillips' Science of Dental Materials. 14th ed. 2026. Ch. 15. Confocal capture may handle reflective surfaces and deep preparations better than triangulation, but comparative studies show variable results [1].

Two more principles BO 6 BO 7

Photogrammetry struggles on teeth but excels on faces and scan bodies

Active wavefront sampling3D in-motion video
1An optical module moves in a circle around the optical axis, sampling the returning light at several positions.
2Depth is rebuilt from how the light wave changed.
3Early systems needed powder; many current versions are powder-free.
4Accuracy depends heavily on calibration.
PhotogrammetryPassive triangulation
1Two or more cameras match natural surface features; no pattern is projected.
2Stereophotogrammetry captures the face for smile design.
3Teeth lack texture and saliva reflects: limited intraoral use.
4Excels at implant verification with high-contrast scan bodies.

Color and wavelength BO 10

Color is not needed to manufacture; it helps you find the margin

MonochromeGeometry only
Shape and nothing else. Sufficient for manufacturing, with the smallest file; the margin must be found from geometry alone.
ColorGeometry plus color
Adds shade, lab communication, documentation, and margin contrast. Costs a larger file and needs calibration.
Beyond visibleOther wavelengths
Some systems add non-visible wavelengths to aid caries detection or to separate tooth from restorative material.

Software processing BO 12

Software repairs the scan, but a filled hole is an estimate

1
Align
Iterative closest point repeatedly minimizes the distance between overlapping surfaces, typically in 20 to 50 iterations.
2
Fix topology
Edges not shared by exactly two faces are resolved; self-intersections are untangled.
3
Fill
Small holes are triangulated across their edges; large gaps are interpolated from the surrounding surface.
4
Clean
Machine learning assists segmentation and cleanup and has made accuracy less sensitive to scanning pattern.
At the margin
A filled hole looks complete, but its shape was calculated, not captured. When the gap is at the finish line, go back and capture it.

Source. [1] Pacheco RR, Rocha MG. Digital technologies. In: Phillips' Science of Dental Materials. 14th ed. Elsevier; 2026. Ch. 15.

Scanning errors BO 13

Most scan defects trace to the operator or to the patient's mouth

Decide where each error comes from. The answer explains what it looks like and what prevents it.

Mesh hole: missing data.
An area the wand never imaged. Check the model before leaving the area.
Stitching error: images misaligned.
Too little overlap or an erratic path. Keep a steady pattern.
Tissue error: cheek, tongue, or lip captured.
Soft tissue was in the field. Retract it, then trim it from the model.
Scanning noise: surface artifacts.
Suboptimal conditions: distance, lighting, or a dirty tip.
Humidity error: saliva or blood.
Fluids absorb or bend light. Isolate, dry, and control bleeding.
Bridge error: adjacent teeth merged.
Too little interproximal space; the software bridges the contact.
Fuzzy finish line: indistinct margin.
An indistinct margin at the tissue; retraction and hemostasis decide.
Implant scanning adds its own error types.
Covered with implant prosthodontics.

Sources. [1] Pacheco RR, Rocha MG. Phillips' Science of Dental Materials. 14th ed. 2026. Ch. 15. [8] Abduo J, Elseyoufi M. Eur J Prosthodont Restor Dent. 2018;26:101-21.

Hybrid workflows BO 15

When the mouth defeats the light, impress first and scan the impression

Complete denturesRemovable
Retention depends on border molding and functional movement. Only an impression records tissue displaced under load.
Full-arch implantsImplant
They need maximum cross-arch accuracy, exactly where stitching error peaks. Impressions or verification jigs are digitized.
Bleeding marginsFixed
Extensive subgingival margins with hemorrhage may require an impression followed by extraoral scanning.

Data fusion BO 18

Merging the scan and the CBCT puts the crown and the bone in one model

Prosthetically driven implant planning designs the restoration first, then places the implant to support it.

1
Surface
The intraoral scan gives tooth morphology and the ideal restoration position.
2
Volume
CBCT gives bone volume and density, root positions, and vital anatomy.
3
Register
Algorithms align the surface to the volume: one digital patient.
4
Transfer
A 3D-printed surgical guide carries the plan to the surgical field.
Also used for endodontic guides for calcified canals, orthognathic planning, and complex rehabilitation.
Limitations: metal artifact scatter, limited soft tissue contrast, and radiation that requires ALARA.

Source. [1] Pacheco RR, Rocha MG. Digital technologies. In: Phillips' Science of Dental Materials. 14th ed. Elsevier; 2026. Ch. 15.
